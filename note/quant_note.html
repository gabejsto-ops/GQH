Trading Like a Bayesian: Confidence-Weighted Time-Series Momentum, and Why It Does Not Beat the Original

Gator Quant Hacks 2026 · Systematic Trading Track · [Team member names] · Code: github.com/gabejsto-ops/GQH (one command: python run_all.py --final)

1. Summary

We start from conservatism: investors update toward news by less than Bayes' rule implies, so prices drift after information and time-series momentum (TSMOM) captures the drift. We tried to use Bayesian reasoning to improve TSMOM on 23 liquid ETFs (2008–2026): first by de-risking only on noisy volatility, then by sizing each position by the posterior probability that its trend is real. We pre-registered every hypothesis in git, ran 15 strategy variants and 3 mechanism tests, held out the last two years and evaluated them once. A naive backtest looks strong: an out-of-sample Sharpe of 1.08 net of costs. Our own audit takes it apart. Measured over cash, it is 0.21 (95% CI −0.72 to 1.58). The Bayesian sizing is indistinguishable from standard TSMOM, and both barely beat simply holding the same vol-targeted portfolio. What survives is the known property of trend-following: a similar long-run Sharpe to long-only (~0.4) with protection in crashes.

2. Economic Hypothesis

Edge source (behavioral). In a Bayesian update, new = old + k(news − old). Conservative investors use a gain below k (Edwards 1968; Barberis, Shleifer & Vishny 1998), so part of each repricing arrives late as drift. Who is on the other side: investors anchored to pre-news valuations, plus hedgers and policy makers who trade gradually for non-profit reasons. Why it persists: the bias is human, and trend-following suffers long whipsaw drawdowns that limit the capital competing it away. The base effect is documented across 58 futures by Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012).

Our extensions (committed before any backtest, commit 06b9b69; Round 2 committed d0e5746 before running). Round 1 (H1/H2): high volatility mixes information shocks (large reliable news, large k, so trends should continue) with noise (small k, so moves reverse). Standard TSMOM cuts size in both; we predicted that cutting only in noisy, inefficient high-vol months would improve it. Round 2 (H4): a Bayesian bets in proportion to confidence. With a flat prior, P(drift > 0) = Φ(z), where z is the t-stat of the trailing mean return, so position = vol-target weight × (2Φ(z) − 1). We predicted higher Sharpe and lower turnover than sign-based TSMOM. Each hypothesis fails if it does not beat S1 net of costs; the mechanism tests fail if the conditional difference is ≤ 0 or insignificant.

3. Data & Universe

Universe (fixed by rule): the most liquid US-listed ETF per distinct exposure with inception before mid-2007: 8 equity (SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA, EEM, EWJ, EWZ, FXI), 6 bond (SHY, IEF, TLT, LQD, HYG, TIP), 5 commodity (GLD, SLV, DBC, USO, DBA), 3 currency (UUP, FXE, FXY), 1 real estate (VNQ). Sources: Yahoo Finance daily bars via yfinance, split- and dividend-adjusted (auto_adjust); risk-free rate = 3-month T-bill (FRED DTB3); factors from the Kenneth French Data Library. We chose public data over the Webull API so judges can reproduce results without credentials. Sample: 2007-04-11 (HYG launch) to 2026-10-02; signals start 2008-05 after a one-year warm-up. Out-of-sample: the last 2 years (2024-10-03 onward), since 20% of 19.5 years is longer than the 2-year cap. The loader drops those rows unless --final is passed.

Data problems. Survivorship: all 23 still trade and were picked knowing they are liquid today; we also excluded UNG by name because we knew its roll losses. Both are hindsight. Corporate actions: adjusted prices; USO's 1-for-8 reverse split (2020-04-28) checked as correctly adjusted. Missing data: none in sample. All four one-day moves above 25% were verified as genuine (EWZ 2008-10-13; USO 2020-03-09 and 2020-04-21; SLV 2026-01-30). Nothing is filled forward.

4. Methodology

Signal and timing. At each month-end close, signal = sign of the trailing L-day return. Trades fill at the next day's open; positions drift with prices until the next rebalance. Sizing (portfolio budget 10% vol split equally, per-asset cap 2× equity, 60-day realized vol): S0 sign only, ±1/N; S1 vol-targeted (standard TSMOM); S2 S1 but sized with 252-day vol in "information-shock" months (21-day vol above its trailing-year 75th percentile and 21-day efficiency ratio |net move| / path length > 0.44, twice the random-walk level 1/√21); S3 S1 × (2Φ(z) − 1), the Bayesian sizing; S4 S1 doubled in calm, efficient months (the low-vol mirror of S2). Thresholds come from theory, not fitting; S3 adds no free parameters. Variants: L ∈ {63, 126, 252} × 5 sizings = 15 strategies, all reported. After Round 2 we selected S3 at the pre-registered L = 252. The lookback was fixed in advance, but the choice of sizing rule depended on results, and we disclose that.

Costs. 5 bps per side on traded notional (commission-free broker; half-spreads are about 1 bp for the largest ETFs and several bps for thin ones such as FXY, DBA and UUP; plus open-auction slippage). Every result is also shown at 10 bps. No borrow cost on shorts or financing on leverage (see §8). Returns are measured in excess of cash: Σi wi(ri − rf), the TSMOM-literature convention. We added this after our audit found the original engine credited T-bill carry (§5).

Tests. Mechanism tests on 4,500 asset-months (outcome = signal × next-month excess return / 60-day vol; Welch and month-clustered OLS): H1 shock vs noisy high-vol months; H3 continuous slope on efficiency; H5a calm efficient vs inefficient. Overfitting controls: Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) over N = 15; 21-day block-bootstrap 95% CIs (2,000 draws); fills delayed by 1 and 4 extra days (lookahead check); a lookback sweep of 21–252 days (plateau check); a long-only baseline (S1's weights, always long); comparison at matched 10% vol; Fama-French 3 + momentum regression (HAC). The engine was verified on synthetic prices (zero weights give zero P&L; a single long position matches buy-and-hold exactly; an asset earning exactly rf shows zero excess return).

What failed (the hypothesis log)

  • H2, S2 shock-aware de-risking: failed. Only 2.3% of asset-months qualify as shocks, so S2 ≈ S1 (Sharpe gap ≤ 0.004).
  • H1, shock months trend more: not significant. 0.034 vs 0.009 (n = 105 vs 995), Welch p = 0.56, clustered p = 0.65.
  • H3, continuous version: failed. Slope −0.02, p = 0.82 (n = 1,100). Out of sample the slope is +0.59, p = 0.006; given the in-sample null, n = 133 and the number of tests run, we treat that as chance.
  • H5, calm efficient trends (S4): failed. H5a 0.027 vs 0.014, p = 0.39; small Sharpe gains come with 30–50% more turnover and vanish at 10 bps.
  • H4, Bayesian sizing (S3): partly supported in-sample (beats S1 at 126 and 252 days, not 63; turnover −37%), then dismantled below.

Tearing down our own headline

  • Cash carry (1.08 → 0.21). The first engine credited bond-ETF interest but paid none on cash. With T-bills at about 4.5% and S3 about 0.8× net long, 3.6 of its 4.5 points of out-of-sample return were the T-bill rate. The apparent improvement out of sample was an artifact of high rates.
  • Size, not skill. S3 runs at lower vol, which flattered its turnover and drawdown. At matched 10% vol, turnover is 10.5× vs 11.6× (−10%, not −37%) and max drawdown −22% vs −25% in-sample, and the drawdown advantage is gone out of sample (−9.7% vs −9.4%). S3 − S1 Sharpe: +0.02 [−0.14, 0.18] in-sample, −0.04 [−0.26, 0.15] out of sample; correlation 0.95–0.98.
  • Not significant. Every interval includes zero (in-sample P(Sharpe ≤ 0) = 0.055, borderline); no Deflated Sharpe passes 0.95.
  • The signal barely beats holding. Long-only with identical vol targeting earns 0.33 in-sample and beats both trend versions out of sample (0.36).
  • Known factors. S3 in-sample loads on momentum (β = 0.09, t = 19) with a small market β of 0.035. Alpha is 0.9%/yr, t = 1.26 (R² = 0.25). Out of sample, market β rises to 0.12 (t = 9.8) and alpha is negative.
  • Regime dependence. Bonds were 60% of in-sample excess P&L (sleeve Sharpe 0.52) and the worst sleeve out of sample (−0.93); out-of-sample gains came from commodities (1.32) and equities (0.66).

What survives. No lookahead: one and four days of extra fill delay lower S3 smoothly (0.39 → 0.37 → 0.36). A plateau: Sharpe 0.30–0.54 for 42–252-day lookbacks (Figure 3). Robust to doubled costs (−0.05). Positive in 12 of 17 calendar years. And the crash profile that separates trend from long-only (excess return, S3 / S1 / long-only): GFC 2008-09 to 2009-03 0.0% / +2.4% vs −5.9%; COVID crash −3.0% / −2.3% vs −12.8%; 2022 +5.9% / +9.9% vs −11.0%. The cost is rebounds: March–June 2009 −3.1% / −5.2% vs +4.6%.

6. Risk Management

Built into the backtest: per-asset vol targeting (10% portfolio budget / 23), a 2× equity cap per asset, monthly rebalancing, 23 assets across 5 classes, and confidence scaling (average gross 0.80×, maximum 2.14×; average net 0.50×). Observed risk (in-sample, S3): daily 99% VaR −0.59%, CVaR −0.79%, worst day −1.42% (2022-11-10), longest drawdown 1,032 trading days. Main weakness found: vol targeting levers low-vol bonds. SHY reached 164% of equity and the bond sleeve 192% gross, so a rate shock is the concentrated risk. Proposed rules for live trading (stated in advance, deliberately not backtested to avoid a 16th trial): (1) gross cap of 100% of equity per asset class and 2.5× in total; (2) if drawdown exceeds 2× the target annual vol, halve the vol target until half the loss is recovered; (3) monitor 1-year rolling market β and cut the equity sleeve if β > 0.3; (4) retire the strategy if its 3-year excess Sharpe is below zero and drawdown exceeds the backtest maximum (−7.1%). Tail: the documented weakness is sharp V-shaped reversals (2009, March 2020), where trend is short at the bottom; we do not hedge it.

7. Liquidity & Capacity

Positions are monthly trades in ETFs whose 2024 median daily dollar volume ranges from about $10M (FXY) to billions (SPY, HYG, LQD). We model cost per trade as fixed 5 bps + square-root impact σdaily·√(trade $ / ADV $), using the trailing 20-day ADV known before the trade. S3's in-sample excess Sharpe (0.44 gross of costs) falls to 0.38 at $1M, 0.33 at $100M, 0.20 at $1B and −0.20 at $10B; out of sample 0.21 → 0.16 at $100M. At $1B the median trade is 1.3% of ADV, but the largest trades in thin names (HYG traded only $3.6M a day in 2008; FXY, DBC and UUP at $10–22M today) exceed daily volume from about $100M, so those trades would need to be split across days. Practical capacity: about $100M, set by the currency and commodity ETFs. Using adjusted prices understates early dollar volume, and ETF creation/redemption adds liquidity our ADV measure misses, so both biases make this estimate conservative.

8. Limitations & Next Steps

  • Short out-of-sample period: 2 years gives a Sharpe standard error of about 0.8; it cannot confirm or reject a 0.4 edge.
  • Selection and hindsight: Round 2 was designed after seeing Round 1; S3 was chosen among sizings after results; the universe reflects 2026 knowledge.
  • Missing costs: ETF borrow fees (FXY, USO and EWZ shorts), leverage financing above the T-bill rate, and wider spreads at the open in stress.
  • Data: Yahoo adjustments rescale history with later dividends (returns unaffected); DTB3 is a discount-basis rate divided by 252.
  • Next: (1) rerun on 1985+ futures, the original universe, to give S3 statistical power; (2) backtest the class caps as a separately pre-registered trial; (3) a 50/50 blend of trend and long-only, since their crisis returns offset; (4) an informative prior that shrinks each asset's drift toward its asset-class mean, a fuller Bayesian version of S3.
